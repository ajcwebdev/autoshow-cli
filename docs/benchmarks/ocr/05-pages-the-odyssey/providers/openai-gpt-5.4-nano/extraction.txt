And ye return in safety? Take my gifts
And loeve my child, in honour of the son

At once the voice of all was to respect
The priest, and to accept the bounteous price;
But if it pleased not Atreus’ mighty son,
Who with rude threatenings stern him thence dismissed.
Beware, old man! that at the’s hollow barks
I find thee not now lingering, or henceforth
Returning, left the garland of thy God
And his briefd receptre should avail thee nought.
I will not loose thy daughter, ’till old age
Steal on her.  From her native country far,
In Argos, in my palace, she shall ply
The loom, and shall be partner of my bed.
Move me no more.  Be gone; hence while thou may’st.
He spake, the old priest trembled and obey’d.
Forlorn he roamed the ocean’s rounding shore,
And, solitary, with much pray’r his King
Bright-haired Latona’s son, Prophus, implored,

Such pray’r he made, and it was heard.  The God,
Down from Olympus with his radiant bow
And his full quiver o’er his shoulder flung,
Marched in his anger; shaken as he moved
His rattling arrows told of his approach.
Gloomy he came as night; fast from the hips
Apart, and sent an arrow. Clang’d the cord
†Dread-lounding, bounding on the silver bow.
Mules first and dogs he struck, but at themselves
Diphancing soon his bitter arrows keen,
Smote them. Death-piles on all sides always blazed.
Nine days throughout the camp his arrows flew;
The tenth, Achilles from all parts convened
The host in council.  Juno the white-armed,
Moved at the fight of Grecians all around
Dying, imparted to his mind the thought.
The full assembly, therefore, now convened,
Uprore Achilles ardent, and began.
Atides! Now, it seems, no course remains
For us, but that the fears roaming again,
We hence return; at least if we survive;
But haste, consult we quick some prophet here
Or priest, or ev’n interpreter of dreams,
(For dreams are also of Jove) that we may learn
By what crime we have thus incited Apollo,
What broken vow, what hecatomb unpaid

He spake and sat, when Thector’s son arose
Calchas, an augur foremost in his art,
Who all things, present, past, and future knew,
And whom his skill in prophecy, a gift
Conferred by Phoebus on him, had advanced
To be conductor of the fleet to Troy;
He, prudent, then admoniishing, replied.
Jove-lord Achilles! Would’st thou learn from me
What cause hath mov’d Apollo to this wrath,
The half-arm’d King? I shall divulge the cause.
But thou, swear first and cov’nant on thy part
That speaking, acting, thou wilt stand prepared
To give me succour; for I judge amis,
Or he who rules the Argives, the supreme
O’er all Achæa’s hold, will be incensed.
Woe to the man who shall provoke the King!
For if, to day, he smother close his wrath,
