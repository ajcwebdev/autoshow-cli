# Mug on the Map

Intro

Teal mug on a folded map, badge facing the windowpane,
ridge still wearing one gray stripe, harbor breathing through the frame.
Papa Bear checks the weather twice; I check the torch again.
We leave before the dusk can teach the ridge to close its hand.

Verse 1

He is human, rust-brown coat, patient where the trail gets steep,
field-guide calm in how he reads the notice, sky, and sleep.
I pack water, spare layer, notebook, torch with extra cells,
two copies of the route in case the breeze rewrites the trail.
Turn-back rule is not a mood; it is printed, plain, agreed.
The mug's enamel weight keeps paper honest in the sea-breeze.
One finger draws the footbridge to the marker near the rise;
I write "Harborlight Camp" where a fresh page learns to organize.
No shortcut gets to vote just because the hillside shines.
A useful question travels lighter than a loud decline.
We clear the table, carry out each wrapper, cap, and crumb;
arrival is the first observation, quiet as it comes.
He says the view can wait for us; I feel my hurry lean.
The ridge holds mist like held breath over harbor-green.
Plan is simple: marked path, clear sky, enough time to stay,
and let an answer show itself instead of dragging it our way.

Chorus

Set the mug down, weigh the map, keep the page from flight.
Name the place, the time, the weather, who was standing by.
Sketch is sketch, not verdict; guess stays dressed as guess.
Describe the evidence first—then decide what it suggests.

Verse 2

At the route board I take a photo, match it to the print;
wooden arrow points to shelter where the ridge begins.
A brighter patch of hillside tries its little counterfeit,
but tempting is not evidence, so we keep the line we picked.
Harbor overlook: the footbridge shrinks from near the shore,
then stretches long from height—same boards, two honest wars.
I draw them both and label where my boots were on the ground;
no sketch has to be wrong for both angles to be sound.
At camp the tent takes level ground, the path stays clear and wide,
telescope asleep in case while tripod feet are tried.
Mug back from the table edge, notebook clipped against the gust;
small order is a lantern before the dark is trusted.
A thin cloud crosses the first star and steals my grand design;
I wanted everything at once, all sky in one straight line.
He says record the cloud as view, not interference with the prize.
So: pale sky, one bright point, water wind—and that suffices.

Chorus

Set the mug down, dim the lamp, let the ridge come through.
Name the place, the time, the weather, what the cloud just did.
Sketch is sketch, not verdict; guess stays dressed as guess.
Describe what changed since last look—then decide what it suggests.

Verse 3

Lantern shield turned to the table, one warm circle, low;
outside it, ridge and sky separate, slow enough to know.
He keeps the glass off sunset's rim till darkness is complete;
patience is an instrument aligned before we see.
At the eyepiece I report a point and not its distance—
bright is what I have; far is still a missing instance.
He asks what's different now; I find the cloud has slid,
harbor lamps gone sharper, pale band thinned above the ridge.
Then a streak cuts open sky while both our eyes are up;
too fast for full path, so we mark direction, time, abrupt.
The notebook leaves a margin where uncertainty can breathe;
a glimpse is not a crown, a flash is not a theory.
We stop talking for a while; quiet joins the watch.
My hands around the teal mug learn the heat of not rushing off.
Last line before the sleeping bags: waiting widened what we saw.
Hurrying would have missed the view and called the missing law.

Bridge

Morning count on the table: mug, notebook, clip, lamp, case.
He scans the square for scraps; we leave no evidence misplaced.
Same marked path to harbor, same bridge, another light—
I add a third small drawing; the first two stay in sight.
Nothing honest gets erased because the angle changed.
Page one holds position, brightness, weather, name.

Chorus

Back by the open window, mug beside the map again.
Four pages, three small footbridges, one habit in the hand:
sketch is sketch, not verdict; guess stays dressed as guess.
Next walk starts where evidence is clear enough to suggest.
