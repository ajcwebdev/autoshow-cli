## Draft

Every live show has its share of hiccups, and the latest episode of JavaScript Jam Live opened with a classic one. The Twitter Space looked like it had started on the host's phone, but nobody else could see it or join. As developer advocate Anthony Campolo explained, it's a quirk many Space hosts eventually run into: sometimes the Space shows as live on your end while it hasn't actually gone public. Wait a minute or two, and it sorts itself out. Patience, as he put it, was a virtue. Co-host Ishan Anand compared the moment to the old Demotivators poster of the Titanic suggesting that maybe your purpose is to serve as a lesson for others. Scott Steinlage summed it up best: no matter how much you know about tech, there's always something waiting to make you look a little foolish.

That honest, good-humored opening set the tone for what turned out to be an exciting announcement: a month-long collaboration between JavaScript Jam and React Miami, plus a brand-new show format called Miami Jam.

What Is JavaScript Jam Live?

JavaScript Jam Live describes itself as an open mic for anything related to JavaScript and web development. If it touches the web platform, it's on topic. The hosts put a strong emphasis on audience participation, and that isn't just a polite gesture. As Scott noted, the most valuable conversations on the show tend to happen when listeners request to speak and bring their own questions, opinions, and experiences. Beginners and seasoned developers are equally welcome, and the hosts actively encourage both to step up to the mic.

The show is run by the team at Edgio, an edge deployment platform. Ishan Anand serves as VP of Product, Anthony Campolo is a developer advocate, and Scott Steinlage is a technical community manager. Together they've built a recurring space where developers can learn from each other in real time.

Meet the React Miami Partners

This episode brought new voices into the mix. Dev Agrawal (who happily answers to Dave as well) joined as a fellow media partner for React Miami alongside Anthony and Scott. Michelle, one of the organizers of React Miami, also dropped in after battling her own Twitter Spaces connection issues, which Dev cheerfully described as just the norm of using Spaces every day.

Michelle shared that she was excited to have Dev, Anthony, and Scott on board as media partners, adding with a laugh that the organizers had essentially given them the green light to do whatever they liked. Anthony's response: we'll see whether you end up regretting it. That kind of trust between conference organizers and community creators is a big part of what makes this collaboration interesting.

Welcome to React Miami Month

Scott playfully renamed the occasion on the spot: it's not React Miami Week, it's React Miami Month. Over the coming weeks, JavaScript Jam will feature speakers from React Miami discussing topics relevant to their talks and work. Importantly, the hosts stressed that the familiar JavaScript Jam vibe isn't going anywhere. Listeners can expect the same open, conversational format they're used to, just with more value: more speakers, more insight into the conference, and a few extra goodies along the way.

Miami Jam: The Speaker Interview, Reinvented

The centerpiece of the announcement was Miami Jam, a show Dev convinced Anthony and Scott to co-host with him. The idea is simple but refreshing. Instead of the typical interview or panel format where a few speakers sit down and answer questions about their talks, Miami Jam is structured like a game show.

Speakers will be asked trivia-style questions. These might be about React itself, odd quirks in JavaScript or TypeScript, or anything adjacent to the React ecosystem. A question could ask a speaker to guess which library is being used in a snippet, or even pose a question about another speaker's topic. The trivia isn't the end goal, though. Each question acts as a springboard into deeper discussion about the speaker's talk, their motivation for giving it, their experiences in the industry, and the projects that led them to the React Miami stage.

In other words, the audience still gets all the insight of a traditional interview, wrapped in a format that's more fun, more interactive, and more collaborative.

Why Gamification Works

There's a good reason this approach is appealing. Standard Q&A formats can feel stiff, with speakers giving rehearsed answers to predictable questions. A trivia challenge introduces a bit of playful pressure and spontaneity. When someone is trying to recall a weird JavaScript coercion rule or identify a library on the fly, their personality comes through. Those unscripted moments often lead to the most memorable stories.

Dev also highlighted a collaborative goal: getting speakers to share experiences with each other, not just with the hosts. When a question about one speaker's topic is directed to another, it naturally sparks cross-conversation and connections that might otherwise only happen in a hallway at the conference.

Putting the Audience in Control

Miami Jam also plans to give the audience a real role. Beyond asking their own questions, attendees may get to engage with speakers in new ways. One example Dev shared: when a question is asked, the audience could decide how many points a speaker earns for their answer. That small mechanic turns passive listeners into active participants with a genuine stake in how the show unfolds.

Takeaways for Community Builders

Whether you run meetups, podcasts, or conference programming, there are a few lessons here worth borrowing. First, technical hiccups happen to everyone, and handling them with humor builds goodwill. Second, partnerships between conferences and community creators can add value for both sides when there's trust and creative freedom. Third, format matters. A small shift, like turning an interview into a game, can make familiar content feel brand new and dramatically increase engagement.

Get Involved

If you want to experience this firsthand, tune into JavaScript Jam Live throughout React Miami Month and keep an eye out for Miami Jam. And remember the hosts' standing invitation: whether you're just starting out or have been building for the web for years, request to come up and join the conversation. Your questions might be exactly what sparks the best discussion of the day.
