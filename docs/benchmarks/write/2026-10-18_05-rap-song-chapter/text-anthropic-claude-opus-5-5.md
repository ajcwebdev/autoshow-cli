# Describe It Before It Means

Intro

Teal mug on a folded map, cream compass facing the window,
Ridge still holding a stripe of mist past the harbor's edge.
You weren't there, so I'm writing it plain, for you, on a fresh page:
Harborlight Camp. Top line. Before we've taken a step.

Verse 1

Papa Bear, field guide, patient, coat the color of rust,
Checks the weather, the trail notice, return time, then he packs up.
Me? Torch, water, notebook, spare layer, checked twice.
Each of us holds a copy of the route. That's the price.
The plan says if the weather turns, we turn. No debate.
The mug's not just for tea, it's a paperweight,
Pins the map when the breeze gets bold,
While we match the harbor footbridge to the first marker post.
At the trailhead I photograph the board, hold it to the print,
Wooden arrow to the sheltered ridge, so that's the way we went.
A shortcut tempts, a brighter hillside flashes gold,
Neither moves us. The agreed route is the route we hold.
At the overlook he shows me: same landmark, new angle.
From the shore the bridge looked short. From up here? Long span.
So I draw it twice and I label where I stood,
Not 'one sketch is wrong.' Both are honest. Both are good.

Chorus

Describe it first, then decide what it means.
Write where you stood, write the weather in between.
Teal mug pinning the page to the table,
Write what you saw. Don't trade the view for a fable.

Verse 2

Camp: tent on the level ground they marked for it,
Path kept clear, telescope still cased while we check the tripod feet.
Mug on the low table, nowhere near the edge,
Notebook clipped so the pages can't go flying off the ledge.
Then a thin cloud slides across the first evening star.
I wanted everything at once. I'm staring, arms crossed, far from calm.
Papa Bear says, 'Record the cloud. It's part of the view.'
So my first entry's small, but every word of it is true.
Dark settles. I lower the lantern, turn the shield to the table,
Little circle of warm light: mug, notebook, enough to be able
To tell the ridge from the sky past the edge of the glow.
He keeps the scope off the sun's last light and waits. Slow.
We trade turns at the eyepiece. I call a bright point bright,
No guessing at the distance. Then he asks, 'What changed since last sight?'
Cloud's moved. Harbor lights sharper, cleaner on the water.
That pale band above the ridge? Faded. Write it. Got it.

Chorus

Describe it first, then decide what it means.
The cloud's in the record now. It's part of the scene.
Teal mug pinning the page to the table,
Write what you saw. Don't trade the view for a fable.

Verse 3

Then a streak cuts the open sky, both of us looking up,
Gone before either one of us could draw the path. Not enough.
So we log the direction, the approximate time,
And the fact it lasted a moment. That's the line.
No 'grand discovery.' The notebook leaves room
For a maybe to stay a maybe instead of a boom.
Then quiet. Nobody talking. The quiet's shared attention.
Hands around the mug, I add one last mention:
Waiting gave us a view that hurrying would've missed.
Before bed we hold the notes up against the route list.
Place, weather, time, observer, named on every page,
Sketches labeled sketches, uncertain left that way.
Morning: mug, notebook, clip, lantern, telescope case, counted.
He sweeps the site for scraps. Clear as we found it.
Same marked path back. Overlook. New light on the span.
I draw the bridge a third time. Nothing erased. That's the plan.

Bridge

Entry one: pale sky. One bright point. Breeze off the water.
Entry: brief streak, direction logged, time approximate. A moment. That's all.
Identification: uncertain. Leave it uncertain.
Observer: Mira. Stood here. Saw this. Nothing further.

Chorus

Describe it first, then decide what it means.
Three bridges on one page, and not one of them erased clean.
Teal mug back by the map at the open window,
Next walk starts with the habit. No bigger story needed to begin, though.
