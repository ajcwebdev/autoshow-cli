# Before You Name the Light

Intro

Teal mug on the map, cream compass to the breeze,
Mist stitched the harbor ridge in one thin seam.
I wrote “Harborlight Camp” on a clean white page;
By dusk, we'd meet the sky—but first, set the stage.

Verse 1

Papa Bear, human guide in a rust-colored coat,
Checked weather, trail notice, return time—then wrote.
I packed torch, water, notebook, spare layer;
We each held the route, so the route stayed shared.
If the weather turned, we'd turn—no pride in the plan;
The mug pinned the map while I traced it by hand.
At the entrance, I matched board, marker, and print;
Took one reference photo, then followed the hint.
A bright hillside tempted; we kept to the track,
No prettier patch was a reason to crack.
From shore, the footbridge looked narrow and short;
From the overlook, long—same bridge, changed report.
I sketched both positions and labeled each view;
Difference didn't mean either drawing untrue.
We pitched on level ground, left the pathway clear;
One cloud crossed one star—I wrote what appeared.

Chorus

Before you name the light, write what you know:
One pale sky, one bright point, one breeze below.
Keep the page open; let uncertainty stay.
Before you name the light, mark where you lay.

Verse 2

Dark settled in; I lowered the lantern's flame,
Turned its shield to the table, tightened the frame.
Warm circle: teal mug, clipped notes, steady hands;
Past its rim, ridge and sky split into bands.
The scope faced away from the sun's final glow;
Papa Bear waited till full dark could show.
We traded the eyepiece. I called it “bright point,”
No guessed-at distance to weaken the joint.
He asked, “What has changed since your previous look?”
The cloud had moved on—I recorded the hook.
Harbor lights sharpened; the pale band withdrew;
Then a brief streak cut clean through the open blue-black.
Too quick for a full path, too real to ignore;
Direction, rough time, brief duration—no more.
We left its identity open, not crowned;
Then waited in quiet with the mug passed around.

Chorus

Before you name the light, write what you know:
A streak, rough time, one direction to show.
Keep the page open; don't widen the claim.
Before you name the light, leave room for no name.

Verse 3

Before sleep, we checked every note with the plan:
Place, weather, time, observer—clear to another hand.
A sketch called a sketch; an unsure label stayed;
No quick glimpse promoted by claims we had made.
Morning: mug, notebook, clip, lantern, case;
I counted each item before clearing the place.
Papa Bear searched the ground for scraps left loose;
Nothing we carried became someone else's refuse.
Same marked path home, harbor opening wide;
At the overlook, dawn changed the bridge's side.
I drew view number three on the very first page;
No old line erased, no correction from age.
Position and light made honest views disagree;
Together, all three showed more than one could see.
Four pages came home—not a grand tale rehearsed:
Describe the evidence. Decide meaning second.

Bridge

Field note—Harborlight Camp. Observer: Mira.
Weather: thin cloud, water breeze growing clearer.
Identification: uncertain. Keep it that way.
Last line: waiting gave the hurried view away.

Chorus

Before you name the light, write what you know:
Three bridge views can differ, and still clearly show.
The teal mug pins the map; the habit now stays:
Describe what the light gives—then weigh what it says.
