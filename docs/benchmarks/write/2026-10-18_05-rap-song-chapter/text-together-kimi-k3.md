# Weight on the Map

Intro

Teal mug on a folded map, cream badge aimed at the glass —
past the harbor, the ridge still wears its thin stripe of mist.
We'd haul the little scope to the marked camp before dusk,
then wait on a clear sky. Careful first. Everything else follows.

Verse 1

Papa Bear — rust-colored coat, field guide, patience in the nickname —
reads the weather, the trail notice, the hour we owe the way back.
I run torch, water, notebook, a spare layer in the pack.
Two copies of the route, one rule that doesn't flex:
if the sky turns, we turn. Said plain, no exceptions.
The mug earns its place — more than tea ever did;
its weight keeps the map flat while the breeze picks at the edges.
Footbridge to first marker, I trace it one finger slow,
then write 'Harborlight Camp' where a fresh page opens.
Table left clear; whatever we carried in goes.
Trailhead: I photograph the route board for reference,
check it against paper — the marker says ridge.
A shortcut sparkles: brighter grass, a quicker promise.
Tempting. But the line we agreed on doesn't budge.
A good observation starts with a careful arrival —
a useful question, and time to let the answer finish.

Chorus

Mug on the map, hold the paper still,
compass badge faced to the windowsill.
Write what you see, not what you want —
first light, low light. Harborlight.

Verse 2

Overlook first. You show me the trick of angles:
footbridge short from the shore, long from above.
I draw it both ways, label where I stood —
no sketch called wrong; the standing place is the caption.
Camp: tent on the level ground they designate,
path kept clear, scope still cased while we test the tripod feet.
Mug on the low table, back from the edge,
notebook clipped shut so the wind can't take a page.
First evening star — and a thin cloud slides across it.
I wanted the whole sky at once. You said:
'Then the cloud is part of the record. Write it in.'
So the first entry runs plain: a pale sky,
one bright point, a breeze coming off the water.
Already the page is useful — it only says
what we could actually see from where we stood.
No verdict yet. Just the view, held steady on paper.

Chorus

Mug on the map, hold the paper still,
compass badge faced to the windowsill.
Cloud in the view is part of the view —
first light, low light. Harborlight.

Verse 3

Dark settles. I drop the lantern, turn its shield to the table —
one warm circle, just enough for the mug and the notebook.
Outside the circle, the ridge and the sky start to separate.
Scope aimed off the last of the glow; we wait for full dark.
We trade the eyepiece. I call a bright point bright —
no guessing its distance. You ask what's changed since last look.
Cloud's moved. Harbor lights sharper. The pale band's faded.
Then — a streak cuts the open sky, both of us looking up.
No time to draw the whole path; it lasted a moment.
So the page gets direction, the rough time, 'gone in a blink.'
A glimpse stays a glimpse; we don't dress it as discovery.
The notebook leaves room for the part we can't prove.
A while, we say nothing — the quiet's part of the watching.
I wrap both hands round the mug and write the last line:
waiting gave us a view that hurrying would have missed.

Bridge

Read it back: place, weather, hour, the name of the watcher.
Sketches labeled as sketches. A maybe stays a maybe,
clear enough for somebody never stood by that scope.
Morning inventory: mug, notebook, clip, lantern, case.
Site swept for scraps — leave it clearer than found.
Same marked path down. Overlook, new angle of light:
a third small drawing. The old two stay. Nothing erased.

Chorus

Mug's home by the map, paper holding still,
four pages, three sketches of one footbridge.
Describe the evidence first; the meaning comes after —
the habit's the find. Harborlight.
