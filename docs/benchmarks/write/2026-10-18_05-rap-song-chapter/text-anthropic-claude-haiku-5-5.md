# Describe It First

Intro

Teal mug on the folded map, its badge facing the open window,
ridge still holding a stripe of morning mist,
Mira with a fresh page, Papa Bear checking the weather once more.

Verse 1

Papa Bear, the field guide in the rust-colored coat, has a patient hand,
checks the weather, the trail notice, the return time,
then Mira counts the torch, the water, the notebook, the spare layer.
Each of them carries a copy of the route and a plan to turn back if the sky turns.
At the trail entrance she photographs the board, checks it against the printed map,
the wooden marker points to the sheltered ridge,
and a tempting shortcut doesn't move them an inch off the agreed path.
At the harbor overlook he shows her the footbridge from a new angle:
short from the shore, long from above.
She draws both views and labels where she stood,
and she won't declare one sketch the wrong one.
At camp the tent goes on level ground, the telescope stays cased,
the mug sits on the low table away from the edge,
a small clip holds the pages down against the breeze off the water.
Thin cloud crosses the first evening star. Mira hoped to see everything at once.
Papa Bear says, record the cloud as part of the view.

Chorus

Describe it first, then decide what it means.
Short from the shore, long from above,
label where you stood and let the sketch keep its place.

Verse 2

Night comes, Mira turns the lantern shield toward the table,
just a warm circle to find the mug and the notebook,
the ridge and the sky start to separate in the dark.
They take turns at the eyepiece. She describes a bright point without guessing its distance.
He asks what has changed since the last look.
The cloud moved, the harbor lights sharpened, the pale band above the ridge faded.
Then a brief streak crosses the open sky while both of them are looking up.
Neither can trace the whole path,
so they write the direction, the approximate time, and that it lasted a moment.
The notebook leaves room for uncertainty.
A quick glimpse does not become a grand discovery on paper.
For a while nobody speaks, and the quiet is part of the shared attention.
Mira warms her hands on the mug and writes one line:
waiting gave them a view they would have missed by hurrying.

Chorus

Describe it first, and leave room to be wrong.
Short from the shore, long from above,
both drawings stay on the page.

Verse 3

Before bed they hold the notebook against the route plan.
The entries name the place, the weather, the time, the observer.
Sketches are labeled as sketches, and the uncertain identification stays uncertain,
so someone who never stood by the telescope can still read it.
Morning: Mira counts the mug, notebook, clip, lantern, and telescope case before packing.
Papa Bear checks the site for scraps and loose gear.
They leave it as clear as they found it
and walk the marked path back toward the harbor.
At the overlook the footbridge looks different again in morning light.
Mira adds a third small drawing to the first page
and erases nothing from the old sketches.
Together the views show how position and light can change an honest observation.
Back at the open window the teal mug returns beside the folded map,
four pages of notes, three sketches of the footbridge,
and one habit: describe the evidence before deciding what it means.

Bridge

Notebook, first evening: a pale sky, one bright point, a breeze off the water.
Notebook, next morning: third drawing of the footbridge, labeled where I stood.
Nothing in the old pages needed erasing.
No verdict, just what we could actually see.

Chorus

Describe it first, then decide, and the decision can change.
Short from the shore, long from above,
both drawings stay, both true from where you stood.
