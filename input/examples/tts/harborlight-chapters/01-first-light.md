Mira arrived at Harborlight before the lamps along the pier went dark. She carried a notebook, a folded map, and a teal enamel mug with a cream mountain-and-compass badge on its side. Papa Bear was already beside the wooden bench, counting the windows in the distant tower.

Their plan was modest: watch the harbor change from morning to night and record what they noticed. Mira drew a line across the first page. Above it she wrote what she expected to see. Below it she left room for surprises.

A fishing boat crossed the reflection of the tower. Its wake broke the straight line of light into a dozen moving pieces. Papa Bear pointed toward the water, but Mira kept her pencil still until the boat had passed. Then she drew the reflection twice, once before the wake and once after it.

They shared warm tea and compared their sketches. The lighthouse had not moved. The water had changed the picture. Mira circled that observation and saved the next page for evening.
