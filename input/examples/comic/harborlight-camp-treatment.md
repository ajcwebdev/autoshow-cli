# Harborlight Camp

## Episode 02: First light

This original treatment follows two adult human characters on a quiet stargazing trip. Papa Bear is the trail name of a patient field guide, not an animal. He has a short gray beard, a rust-orange coat, dark trousers, and a canvas daypack. His canonical speaker key is papa-bear. Mira is an adult sketcher with a dark braid, a teal field jacket, round gold glasses, and a small notebook. Their shared prop is a teal enamel travel mug with a cream compass-and-mountain badge. The story has 24 distinct visual beats for adaptation into 20-25 panels.

1. Morning light enters a small harbor workshop. Mira places the teal mug on a folded map while Papa Bear checks the weather notice beside the window. Boats and the low footbridge sit beyond the glass.

2. A breeze lifts one corner of the map. Mira steadies it with the mug and traces the marked route toward Harborlight Camp. The cream badge faces the reader, establishing a prop that will stay recognizable throughout the trip.

3. Papa Bear checks the return time, water, torch, and spare layer. Mira clips a blank notebook page to its backing. Their preparations are practical and calm rather than a hurried departure.

4. Papa Bear says, "We do not need to see everything tonight. We only need time to notice what is here." Mira closes the map and puts it in the outer pocket of her pack.

5. At the trail entrance, the two compare the printed route with a wooden marker. Mira's jacket and glasses match the reference images. A distant shortcut is visible, but neither character steps onto it.

6. The harbor footbridge appears below them as they reach the overlook. Mira begins a small sketch. Papa Bear waits beside the marker while the workshop window becomes a tiny square across the water.

### The ridge before dusk

7. Mira holds the sketch beside the real bridge. Its perspective surprises her: a shape that seemed short from the shore stretches across the page from above. Papa Bear points toward her standing place rather than correcting the drawing.

8. Papa Bear says, "Write down where you are looking from. A different view can still be an honest view." Mira labels the sketch with the overlook name and closes the notebook.

9. They arrive at the designated campsite. A level patch holds the teal tent, a low wooden table, and a clear walking path. Papa Bear lays the telescope case down while Mira checks that no strap crosses the path.

10. Together they unfold the tripod. Mira holds its center while Papa Bear checks each foot. The movement is deliberate enough to give several useful changes of hand position and camera angle.

11. Mira sets the mug and clipped notebook on the table. The lantern remains unlit. The harbor lights begin to appear behind the lower ridge, and the last warm color reaches the tent door.

12. A narrow cloud crosses the first bright point in the sky. Mira lowers her pencil, disappointed that the view is changing. Papa Bear watches the same cloud without treating it as a failure.

<!-- pagebreak -->

### A smaller circle of light

13. Papa Bear says, "Put the cloud in the notes too. We came to watch the sky we have, not the sky we imagined." Mira writes a short entry about the breeze, cloud, and bright point above the ridge.

14. Mira lights the lantern too brightly at first. Its glare fills her glasses and hides the faint outline of the hills. She notices the problem before either person looks through the telescope.

15. Papa Bear turns the lantern shield toward the table. He says, "A small circle of light is enough for the page. Leave the rest of the night room to be seen." The mug and notebook remain softly lit.

16. With the lantern lowered, the ridge becomes distinct against the sky. Mira looks up and smiles. The composition repeats the earlier campsite view, now with a dark blue sky and a much smaller warm light.

17. They take turns at the eyepiece. Mira describes a bright point, then checks the notebook. Papa Bear listens without supplying an elaborate explanation. The attention stays on what each observer can actually report.

18. A faint streak crosses the open sky while both characters happen to be looking upward. The image shows a brief narrow line, not an explosion or a supernatural event. Their shared surprise is quiet and unmistakable.

### What they bring home

19. Mira quickly marks the streak's direction and approximate time. The sketch is incomplete because the event was brief. Papa Bear leaves room beside the drawing for a question rather than filling it with a confident answer.

20. Papa Bear says, "You can leave a question on the page. Good notes do not have to pretend they know more than we saw." Mira adds a small question mark and closes the clip over the paper.

21. The pair sit beside the low table for a silent moment. Mira warms her hands around the teal mug. The tent, telescope, and lantern form the same stable shapes as the style seed, with no extra people or creatures in the campsite.

22. Morning light returns. Mira counts the mug, notebook, clip, lantern, and telescope case as they pack. Papa Bear checks the campsite for scraps. They leave the designated ground clear and follow the marked path home.

23. At the overlook, Mira adds a second bridge sketch in the new light. Papa Bear says, "The walk is the same. The light has changed. Keep both views." The earlier sketch remains visible rather than being crossed out.

24. Back in the workshop, the mug again anchors the folded map. Mira opens the notebook to a final line: "Describe the evidence before deciding what it means." Through the window, the ridge waits in clear morning light. The ending echoes the opening without implying that the trip never happened.
